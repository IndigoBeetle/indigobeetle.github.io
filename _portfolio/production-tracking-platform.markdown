At Rushes Postproduction, a Soho film, television and visual effects facility,
I moved beyond my core 3D rendering responsibilities to solve a wider
production workflow problem. I learned the team's processes and existing
software, then designed and implemented a bespoke shot-tracking system from
scratch.

The project required close collaboration with production staff and artists.
The useful requirements were found in their day-to-day work rather than in a
fixed technical specification, so the solution had to fit the language and
rhythm of an established creative pipeline.

This is representative of the work I enjoy most: understanding a specialist
domain, making a complicated process easier to manage, and delivering software
that supports people without getting in their way.

It also shows a recurring strength in my work: being able to enter an unfamiliar
business domain, learn enough of the operational reality to make sensible
technical decisions, and produce a tool that is useful to the people doing the
work every day.
